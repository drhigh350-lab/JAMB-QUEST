# JAMB Quest Game Arcade — Three-Mode Blueprint

## Design Intent

The arcade must feel like **three real games powered by JAMB mastery**, rather than three quiz skins. Questions remain the non-negotiable engine: a correct response can unlock progress, while a miss becomes a visible repair opportunity. The player’s route, project, tool choice, and recovery decision must change what happens next.

> **Design rule:** no mode may use paid currency, random chance rewards, answer alteration, a timer as its primary loop, or a failure state that prevents correction and return.

## Research Translation

| Research finding | Arcade rule |
|---|---|
| A resource loop needs clear sources and sinks, with a visible balanced progression path. [1] | Each game states what questions earn, what choices spend, and what lasting project or route result follows. |
| Presidency simulators retain attention through projects, advisers, dashboards, and consequential events. [2] | President’s Desk uses a fictional nation, transparent department projects, and visible civic indicators—but no real parties, leaders, or political persuasion. |
| Meaningful educational play requires discernible feedback, agency, and consequences that are neither trivial nor relentlessly critical. [3] | Every project/tool decision has a clear immediate effect and a later consequence; a miss creates a repair path rather than a hard lock. |
| Learning games should balance engagement, assessment, and learning—not bolt points onto disconnected exercises. [4] | All modes retain approved-bank selection, explanations, missed-card review, and evidence that can guide the learner’s next study action. |

## Shared Arcade Foundation

All modes will launch from one **Game Arcade** desk at the end of Practice. The shared foundation provides a local player profile, a safe pause/exit, optional sound/motion preferences, card-level explanation feedback, and a hand-off into the existing correction flow. Each mode stores separate local progress and never creates a CBT record or changes a question’s answer, topic, explanation, or availability.

| Shared element | Learner-facing meaning | Integrity safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Mastery tokens | Earned only from a correct approved question; used for a visible mode decision. | Never purchasable, never random, never interchangeable with marks. |
| Repair cards | A missed question enters a small correction queue with its real explanation. | A repair remains a real question, not a guessed recap. |
| Route/project ledger | Shows decisions, performance, and next move. | Clearly labelled as game progress, not a prediction of JAMB score. |
| Subject territories | Keep every session centred on the learner’s chosen subject or balanced set. | Draw only active approved learner questions. |

## Mode 1 — Study Expedition

**Fantasy:** The learner maps four subject territories and secures study routes. They choose a 5-, 10-, or 15-card contract and one transparent tool before beginning. Correct cards secure map segments and award territory stamps; misses become repair cards. This mode already exists and will be retained as the route-collection pillar of the arcade.

## Mode 2 — President’s Desk: The Commonwealth Briefing

**Fantasy:** The learner leads a fictional, non-partisan country called **Asterra**. Their cabinet has a modest treasury, public confidence, and four capability tracks: Education, Health, Energy, and Innovation. They select one public project at the start of a turn—for example, equip a science laboratory, repair a power grid, train teachers, or fund a community health clinic.

Questions appear as **cabinet briefings**. A correct answer funds the selected project and awards policy insight. A miss creates a small, transparent setback such as a delayed project segment or reduced public confidence, then immediately opens an explanation and a repair option. No real-world politician, party, country, war, electoral persuasion, or exploitative political content is used.

| Turn step | Meaningful player choice | Question-powered result |
|---|---|---|
| Choose a ministry project | Trade a smaller immediate gain against a larger multi-card project. | The chosen project determines which dashboard result will change. |
| Select one adviser tool | Pick clarity, resilience, or research support once per turn. | The tool changes a transparent scoring/recovery effect, never the correct answer. |
| Resolve cabinet briefings | Answer approved questions in the chosen territory. | Correct answers generate treasury and insight; misses create repair cards. |
| Deliver a term report | Choose whether to finish a project or repair a weak briefing set. | The next turn’s options respond to actual accuracy and repair completion. |

## Mode 3 — The Great Archive: Architects of Knowledge

**Fantasy:** A four-wing archive has lost its knowledge mosaics. The learner becomes the Chief Archivist, rebuilding the English, Biology, Chemistry, and Physics halls. Each game begins with a meaningful blueprint choice: **Balanced Wing** requires a set across subjects, **Mastery Gallery** asks for a deeper same-subject collection, and **Repair Vault** turns recent misses into a rebuild mission.

Each correct question produces a visible knowledge tile matching its subject and official topic. Tiles can be placed only where the selected blueprint permits, so a learner decides whether to diversify, pursue a difficult topic cluster, or clear a repair set. A missed card is placed on the Repair Bench with its explanation; restoring it on a later revisit completes a special mosaic segment. The final archive board becomes a truthful visual record of what the learner has practised and repaired.

| Blueprint | Strategy | Educational purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Balanced Wing | Collect a useful spread across four territories. | Encourages sustainable full-subject preparation. |
| Mastery Gallery | Build one topic or subject collection with escalating contracts. | Supports deliberate focused practice. |
| Repair Vault | Restore previously missed cards with explanation-led retries. | Converts error correction into a visible completion loop. |

## Build Sequence

The immediate work is to finish the shared arcade entry and deliver **President’s Desk** as the first new substantial mode. Study Expedition will move into that arcade shell; The Great Archive follows once the shared local profile, repair-card queue, and mode analytics are proven reliable.
