# Arcade Game-Loop Research — 23 August 2026

JAMB Quest should avoid a shallow points overlay. Ohio State’s gameful-learning guidance identifies learner choice, ongoing feedback, monitored progress, and a reduced fear of failure as the differentiators from traditional delivery. Its practical strategies include flexible task variety, clear learning goals, timely feedback, exploration where failure is treated as a learning moment, and identity play.[1]

The Arcade rebuild will translate these principles into visible, non-random world change: a learner chooses a mission, sees the immediate result of a correct or missed card, completes an honest objective, and receives an exact repair route rather than a punishment. Return motivation will come from unfinished world goals, newly available mission variants, and scheduled repair opportunities—not cash, paid advantages, variable-ratio rewards, loss-aversion streaks, or claims of mastery.

An ACM study of a spaced, interleaved retrieval-practice tool gives a practical model for return loops: it used a visible evolving schedule, meaningful credit for returning on separate days, and gameful design around scheduled topics rather than random individual cards. The study warns that spacing and retrieval can feel harder in the short term, so the interface must make the long-term route visible rather than disguising it with superficial rewards.[2] A 2024 systematic review and meta-analysis similarly found that gamification can support intrinsic motivation, autonomy, and relatedness, while warning that effects vary by design and that competence must be supported rather than presumed.[3]

Accordingly, the rebuilt Arcade will use: (1) a visible district/expedition/archive board with genuine world state; (2) chosen missions with clear ends; (3) distinct short-session goals such as secure a route, restore an artefact, or complete a civic build; (4) a repair lane that converts misses into a recoverable world task; and (5) a return board that shows the next available study mission. It will not use random prize chests, cash, paid power, leaderboard pressure, streak loss, or claims of mastery. The initial 2024 digital-educational-games review located in the search was unavailable in the browser because of a verification wall, so it is not used as a sole product-evidence source.[4]
